Most SEO checklists have fifty items and no prioritization. For an early-stage startup with limited time, that's worse than no checklist at all. Here's the shorter, ordered version: fix these first, in this order, before anything else.

## 1. Make sure the basics actually exist

This sounds too obvious to mention, and it's the single most common issue we find auditing real sites: no `robots.txt`, no `sitemap.xml`, a broken Open Graph image reference that's been silently killing every social share preview for months. None of this requires strategy — it requires checking that the files exist and the URLs they reference actually resolve.

## 2. Fix your metadata before you write more content

A precise, specific `<title>` and meta description on every page beats another blog post nobody finds. If your title tag still says "Home" or your homepage description is generic boilerplate, that's a five-minute fix with outsized return relative to the effort.

## 3. Add structured data where it's true

`Organization` schema for your company, `SoftwareApplication` or `Product` schema for what you sell, `FAQPage` if you have real FAQ content. Structured data doesn't directly move rankings much on its own, but it materially improves how you show up in results — and increasingly, how accurately AI answer engines describe you.

## 4. One clear h1, logical heading structure

Every page should have exactly one `<h1>` that states what the page is about, with `<h2>`s and `<h3>`s that nest logically underneath. This is a five-minute audit that a surprising number of startup sites fail.

## 5. Publish content with a real point of view, on a schedule you can sustain

This is the slow-compounding item, and it's where most startups either give up or burn out with an unsustainable pace. Better to publish one genuinely useful, specific post every two weeks than five generic ones in a sprint and then nothing for six months. Search engines and AI answer engines both reward consistency and specificity over volume.

## 6. Track qualified traffic, not just impressions

Impressions are easy to inflate and mean very little. What matters is whether the people finding you are the people you're trying to reach — that requires actually reading who's showing up, not just how many.

## What to skip, for now

Backlink outreach campaigns, keyword-stuffing on low-value pages, and chasing every algorithm-update headline are all lower priority than the six items above for an early-stage company. Get the fundamentals right first; the advanced tactics only pay off once the foundation is solid.
