Marketing agencies used to be the default move for a founder who didn't have time to do growth themselves. That default is breaking down — not because agencies got worse, but because the gap between what they can deliver and what's now possible got a lot wider.

## The agency model has structural limits

A good agency brings expertise and execution capacity you don't have in-house. It also brings real constraints that don't show up in the pitch deck:

- **Latency.** A retainer relationship runs on a cadence of calls and revisions, not real-time reaction to what's happening in your business.
- **Context loss.** An external team never has the context a founder has — what a specific customer actually said, why a decision got made, what's about to ship. Every brief is a lossy compression of that context.
- **Cost that scales with headcount, not output.** You're paying for hours and seniority, not directly for results, and the incentive to expand scope (and retainer) is built into the relationship.

None of this makes agencies bad. It makes them a specific tool with specific tradeoffs — good for campaigns that need deep specialized craft, worse for the constant, context-heavy work of everyday growth.

## What changed

The work that used to require a team — research, writing, distribution, analysis, iteration — can now be done by a system that has full, constant context on the business because it's talking to the founder directly, not receiving a monthly brief. That closes the latency and context-loss problems agencies structurally can't solve, without needing to build an in-house team either.

## This isn't "AI replaces marketers"

It's narrower than that: AI replaces the parts of the agency relationship that were really about coordination and execution overhead, not craft. Founders still need judgment — is this positioning right, is this the story worth telling — and increasingly that's the only part of growth they're spending time on, because a growth intelligence with real context on the business does the surrounding work.

That's the shift Collision is built around: instead of briefing an external team once a month, you talk to a system that already has the context, every day.
