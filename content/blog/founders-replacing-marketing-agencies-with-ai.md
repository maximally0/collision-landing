Marketing agencies used to be the default move for a founder who didn't have the time or expertise to run growth themselves. That default is breaking down — not because agencies suddenly got worse at their craft, but because the gap between what they can realistically deliver and what's now possible with a different approach got a lot wider, quickly.

## The agency model has structural limits that aren't about talent

A good agency brings genuine expertise and execution capacity a founder often doesn't have in-house, and dismissing that would be dishonest. It also brings real structural constraints that don't show up anywhere in the pitch deck, because they're inherent to the relationship, not to the specific people involved:

- **Latency.** A retainer relationship runs on a cadence of scheduled calls and batched revisions, not real-time reaction to what's actually happening in the business day to day. By the time a brief is written, reviewed, executed, and returned, the moment it was meant to capture has often passed.
- **Context loss.** An external team, no matter how good, never has the context a founder has by default — what a specific customer actually said on a call last Tuesday, why a particular decision got made and what tradeoff it resolved, what's about to ship next sprint. Every brief is necessarily a lossy compression of that context, filtered through whatever the founder remembered to mention and had time to write down.
- **Cost that scales with headcount and seniority, not with output.** You're paying for hours and experience level, not directly for outcomes, and the built-in incentive in most retainer structures is to expand scope — and therefore the retainer — over time, independent of whether growth actually accelerated.

None of this makes agencies a bad choice categorically. It makes them a specific tool with specific, well-understood tradeoffs: strong for campaigns that genuinely need deep specialized craft delivered by senior practitioners, weaker for the constant, context-heavy, low-latency work that day-to-day growth actually requires.

## What actually changed

The work that used to require assembling a team — research, writing, distribution, performance analysis, iteration — can now be done by a system that maintains full, constant context on the business because it's in direct, ongoing conversation with the founder, rather than receiving a monthly brief filtered through whatever made it into a Notion doc. That directly addresses both the latency problem and the context-loss problem that agencies are structurally unable to solve, regardless of how talented the people staffed on the account are — without requiring the founder to build and manage an in-house team either, which carries its own hiring, management, and retention overhead.

## This isn't "AI replaces marketers" as a blanket claim

It's narrower and more specific than that framing suggests: AI replaces the parts of the agency relationship that were really about coordination overhead and execution throughput, not the parts that were genuinely about craft or strategic judgment. Founders still need to exercise judgment — is this specific positioning right for this specific moment, is this the story actually worth telling right now — and increasingly, that judgment call is the only thing founders using this approach are spending their limited time on, because a growth system with constant, real context on the business handles the surrounding research, drafting, and distribution work that used to consume most of the relationship's bandwidth.

## What this means practically

For a founder deciding between hiring an agency, building an in-house team, or adopting an AI growth system, the honest framing is: agencies remain a reasonable choice for discrete, high-craft campaigns with a defined scope and timeline. In-house teams remain the right call once growth work is large and specialized enough to justify permanent headcount. Everything in between — the continuous, context-heavy, cross-channel work that most early and growth-stage companies actually need most of the time — is exactly the gap an AI growth system is positioned to fill, because it's the gap defined by needing constant context and low latency, which is precisely what the traditional agency retainer structure cannot provide by design.
