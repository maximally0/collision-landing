For twenty years, "getting found" meant one thing: ranking on Google. That's no longer true.

A growing share of research now starts — and ends — inside an AI answer engine. Someone asks ChatGPT to compare tools in your category, or asks Perplexity what the best option is for a specific job-to-be-done, and they get a synthesized answer with a short list of sources. If you're not one of those sources, you don't exist in that conversation.

## AEO is not SEO with a new name

Search engine optimization was built around a ranking algorithm that returns ten blue links. Answer engine optimization is built around a language model that reads your content, decides whether it's trustworthy and specific enough to cite, and then paraphrases it into a direct answer.

That changes what "optimized" content looks like:

- **Specific claims beat vague positioning.** A model can't cite "we're the best solution for X" — it can cite a concrete fact, number, or mechanism.
- **Structured data matters more, not less.** JSON-LD, clear headings, and FAQ sections give a crawler unambiguous signal about what your page is actually about.
- **A clean llms.txt helps.** The emerging convention lets you hand AI crawlers a concise, structured summary instead of making them parse marketing copy full of animations and calls to action.

## What we did on usecollision.com

We treated our own site as the first test case: added Organization, SoftwareApplication, and FAQPage structured data, published a `llms.txt`, and made sure every schema claim has a matching visible section on the page — because answer engines (like search engines) discount structured data that doesn't match what's actually shown to a visitor.

The bigger shift is upstream of any one page, though. If your content strategy still treats "SEO" and "everything else" as separate buckets, you're already behind — the surfaces where people look for answers just multiplied, and your growth engine needs to publish to all of them, coherently, without becoming ten disconnected side projects.
