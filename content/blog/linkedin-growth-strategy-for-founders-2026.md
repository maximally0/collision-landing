Most LinkedIn advice stops at "post consistently." That's necessary but nowhere near sufficient — plenty of founders post every day and go nowhere. Here's what actually separates accounts that compound from ones that plateau.

## Specificity beats frequency

The algorithm rewards engagement, but engagement follows specificity. "5 tips for better productivity" gets skimmed and ignored. "We lost our biggest customer because of a decision I made in week 3, here's what I got wrong" gets read to the end. Founders with real reach almost always write from a specific, sometimes uncomfortable, first-person vantage point — not generic advice content.

## The comment section is half the job

Posting is the visible half of LinkedIn growth. Replying — to comments on your own posts, and thoughtfully to other people's posts in your space — is the other half, and it's the one most founders skip because it doesn't feel like "real" work. It is. Early reach on LinkedIn comes disproportionately from conversation, not broadcast.

## Consistency compounds, virality doesn't

A single viral post gives you a spike and a lot of low-intent followers who churn out of your feed within a week. A steady cadence of specific, on-topic posts builds an audience that actually recognizes your name six months later — which is the audience that turns into inbound. Optimize for the second one.

## What this looks like operationally

For a founder without a content team, the real question isn't strategy — most founders already know what "good LinkedIn" looks like. It's throughput: can you consistently turn what's actually happening in the business (a hard decision, a customer conversation, a mistake, a small win) into a post, several times a week, without it eating your whole afternoon?
