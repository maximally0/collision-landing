If you've been reading about AI marketing teams, you've seen Epiminds. It's the Stockholm startup that raised a $6.6M seed led by Lightspeed in October 2025, got covered in Forbes and Business Insider, and built the pitch that sounds almost exactly like Collision's: an AI marketing department in a box, where one orchestrator — they call her Lucy — coordinates a team of 20+ specialized agents that analyze, execute and report on campaigns.

We're not going to pretend it doesn't matter. It does. It's the strongest conceptual twin in the market, and it's worth understanding precisely because it's positioned on the same category narrative we're building on. Here's the honest look.

## What Epiminds does well

The architecture is genuinely interesting: a named orchestrator (Lucy) coordinating specialized agents across data, creative and strategy — agents for campaign analysis, budget and bid optimization, creative analysis, reporting, and ad execution on Google, Meta and TikTok. Execution runs through a human-approval workflow, which is the right default for anything that spends money in your name.

The funding is real, the coverage is real, and the product targets a serious buyer: marketing agencies and in-house marketing teams that need campaign operations at scale. Lightspeed money buys distribution, and the "operating system for marketing" framing is a category-defining claim.

## The honest assessment

Here's what the DB says and what it means (assessment where it's judgment):

- **It's early.** Founded 2025. Pricing is unpublished — the company is doing pilot and agency deals, not listed plans. The seed round is October 2025, so we're looking at a product that's been in the world for months.
- **It's ads-centric.** Its agents are built around paid campaign operations: analysis, budget/bid optimization, creative analysis, reporting. There's no SEO or AI-search engine in the picture, no organic content loop, no outbound. The DB marks the overlap assessment as: strong on the "replace the marketing team" budget, but a paid-media operations layer today.
- **It sells to teams, not founders.** Its buyer is an agency or an in-house marketing team with campaigns to run — not a founder with no team at all.
- **The threat is real and specific.** The funding and the narrative could pull the buyers Collision wants toward "just buy the AI team." That's the exact conversation we want to be in — and Epiminds is making it louder.

## Who should choose Epiminds

- You're an agency or a marketing team running paid campaigns across Google, Meta and TikTok and want an operations layer on top.
- You want an AI team that plugs into campaign execution specifically — and you're comfortable with a pilot relationship and unpublished pricing.
- You're investing in the "AI marketing department" narrative early and are fine with early-stage tradeoffs.

## Who should choose Collision instead

- You're a founder who doesn't have an in-house team — you need the whole loop, not an ads operations layer. Collision covers strategy, SEO/AI search, LinkedIn, X, email and outbound, with one memory so the strategy conversation on Tuesday shapes the posts on Friday.
- You care about organic growth: SEO and being cited in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews. That's the surface Epiminds doesn't touch.
- You want approval-based execution — nothing ships without your sign-off — across *every* action, not just ad spends.

## The bigger picture

Epiminds validates the category Collision is built for: people genuinely want to hire an AI marketing team. But today it's an early, ads-centric operations layer for teams that already exist — while Collision is built as the intelligence a founder hires to run growth end-to-end. Watch Epiminds closely; it has money, momentum and the exact same narrative. For a founder without a team, though, it's not the same product.
