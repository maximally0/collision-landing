An AI CMO is a system of AI agents that takes on the work a marketing leader owns: understanding the market, setting strategy, producing work across channels, shipping it, measuring it, and deciding what to do next. The category went from a thought experiment to a funded market in roughly a year — 2026 is the year it's being sold.

## What an AI CMO actually does

A human CMO runs five functions. AI CMO products map to the same five layers, with different depth per product:

1. **Research** — market intelligence, competitor analysis, audience insight
2. **Strategy** — positioning, channel choice, campaign planning
3. **Execution** — content, campaigns, outbound, ads, social
4. **Measurement** — performance, attribution, reporting
5. **Optimization** — what changed as a result, and what happens next

Products differ mainly in **which layers they automate and how much of the loop closes**. Some are drafting layers for content. Some are campaign execution systems with real send infrastructure. Some are positioned as full growth teams. The honest differences show up in one place: how much of the loop runs without you, and how much still needs a human team around the tool.

## The five questions that expose what you're really buying

1. **Does it draft, or does it execute?** A drafting layer still leaves distribution to you. Ask what you'd still do yourself after buying it.
2. **Does it remember?** Most tools have per-task context. The expensive part of marketing isn't producing one asset — it's coordination. Ask whether Tuesday's strategy decision shapes Friday's posts.
3. **Who approves?** Fully autonomous posting is how brands get embarrassed. Approval gates are a feature, not a concession.
4. **Does it learn?** Does the system measure results and change its next move, or does it just produce more output?
5. **What does it replace?** Point tools replace point tools. A real AI CMO replaces the coordination cost — the stack of 10 tools plus freelancers plus agencies that never share context.

## AI CMO vs what came before

The category didn't invent new capabilities. It re-bundled them. A 2026 AI CMO platform does what previously took an SEO tool, a writing tool, a social scheduler, an email platform, an analytics suite, a freelancer and a strategist — none of which talked to each other. The value claim isn't "AI writes content." It's "one system with one memory runs the whole loop, and nothing ships without your approval."

That's also the honest limitation: no product in the category can replace judgment, taste, or customer relationships. The products that pretend they can are the ones to avoid.
