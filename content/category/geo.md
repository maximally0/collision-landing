# Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)

## What is GEO?

Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the practice of making your brand the answer that AI search engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Copilot — cite when people ask questions. Where SEO wins rankings, GEO wins citations and mentions inside AI-composed answers.

## Why does GEO matter in 2026?

Because AI engines now sit between buyers and your website. A meaningful and growing share of product research starts with a question typed into ChatGPT or Perplexity — and when the answer is assembled, the engines cite the sources they trust. If your brand isn't in that answer, you're not in the consideration set, and no Google ranking fixes it.

Three shifts make 2026 the year GEO became a job:

1. **AI Overviews** sit on top of Google's results, absorbing clicks that used to go to organic listings.
2. **ChatGPT and Perplexity** are now default research surfaces for B2B buyers — they ask before they search.
3. **The measurement industry arrived.** Platforms track real query volumes in the hundreds of millions (Profound tracks 200M+), and tens of thousands of marketers now watch AI citations as a KPI. This isn't a fad; it's a distribution surface with its own instruments.

## How does GEO work?

Six steps:

1. **Measure your current presence.** Track where your brand is cited — or absent — across ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Overviews, Gemini and Copilot. Platforms like Otterly and Profound do this engine by engine.
2. **Find the questions.** Research the actual queries buyers put to AI engines in your category — the language, the follow-ups, the comparisons.
3. **Make your content answerable.** AI engines quote content that stands alone: a direct answer in the first paragraph, specific numbers, quotable claims, clear entities. Structure pages so the answer is extractable.
4. **Earn citation-worthy sources.** Engines favor sources that are consistent across the web, linked by authoritative sites, and clearly attributed to a real brand.
5. **Publish, refresh, repeat.** AI engines reward fresh, consistent content. A one-time post decays; a maintained answer compounds.
6. **Watch share-of-voice.** Track citations per engine against competitors, and feed what works back into the next round.

## What are the common GEO mistakes?

- **Optimizing for Google only.** GEO is not SEO with a new label — the engines read differently, weight sources differently and answer differently.
- **Writing for humans and hoping.** The answer must be extractable: first-paragraph answers, numbers, headings, lists.
- **Ignoring citations.** It's not enough to be mentioned — engines cite sources they trust, and trust is built with links, consistency and authority.
- **One-shot content.** GEO is a loop: publish, measure, refresh. Content that goes stale stops getting cited.
- **Never looking at the answers.** If you don't know what engines currently say about you, you can't steer it. Monitoring is the prerequisite for optimization.

## FAQ

**Is GEO the same as SEO?** No. SEO optimizes for a ranked list of links; GEO optimizes for the citations inside an AI-composed answer. Related games, different rules.

**Does GEO matter for B2B?** More than most. B2B buyers research with AI engines before they talk to sales — being the cited answer is being in the deal.

**How long until GEO shows results?** Weeks, not days. Citation shifts lag content changes, so expect the first measurable movement in 4–8 weeks of consistent publishing.

**Do I need a dedicated GEO platform?** If monitoring is your only need, yes — Profound and Otterly are excellent. If you need monitoring plus the writing and distribution that actually move citations, you need an intelligence, not an instrument.
